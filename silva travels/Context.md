## Product Overview

**One-liner:**  
Silva Travel is an IATA-accredited, Auckland-based travel agency providing expert international flight bookings, worldwide hotel accommodations, complex visa guidance, and dedicated human support—specializing in travel between New Zealand and India, Australia, Fiji, Sri Lanka, the Middle East, and global destinations.

**What it does:**  
Silva Travel (Silva Travel & Tours Ltd.) bridges the gap between impersonal online booking engines and stressful long-haul international travel. Operating from their physical office on Sandringham Road in Auckland and online at silvatravel.co.nz, their experienced travel consultants compare airlines, flight routes, stopovers, baggage allowances, and fare conditions to assemble transparent, cost-effective travel packages. Beyond ticketing, Silva Travel provides comprehensive visa guidance (identifying transit visa requirements and assisting with tourist visas), manages date changes and reissues, and remains on-hand with 24/7 disruption support before, during, and after travel.

**Product category:**  
Auckland travel agency, international flight booking specialist, New Zealand to India travel agency, IATA-accredited travel agent, visa guidance & assistance NZ, trans-Tasman & Pacific holiday planner, corporate travel management.

**Product type:**  
Full-service travel agency and bespoke travel advisory (hybrid: Auckland walk-in retail office + online quotation and booking portal).

**Business model:**  
Full-service agency earning revenue through negotiated airline net fares, unpublished consolidated airfare inventory, airline partner commissions, hotel and transfer package margins, and transparent service fees for complex itinerary curation, visa consultations, and ticket reissues/cancellations.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:**  
Long-haul international travel from New Zealand (often 24–35 hours door-to-door) is complicated, high-stakes, and increasingly expensive. Online travel aggregators provide misleading teaser prices, conceal strict baggage caps, fail to check transit visa rules, and completely abandon passengers when flights are cancelled or delayed.

**Why alternatives fall short:**  
- **Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) & Aggregators:** Lure travellers with low base fares that explode after adding baggage, seat selection, and credit card surcharges; when disruptions occur, customers face unreachable offshore chatbots and hours on hold.
- **Direct Airline Websites:** Airlines only sell their own metal or alliance partners, meaning they cannot compare alternative carriers or combine the best outbound and return deals across multiple airlines.
- **Generic Travel Agencies:** Often lack deep, specialized knowledge of subcontinental routes (e.g., regional airports in India like Amritsar or Kochi), transit visa nuances through third-party countries, or the luggage requirements of diaspora families.
- **DIY Online Visa Portals:** Filled with outdated information, confusing official portals, and scam websites that cause travellers to apply for incorrect visa categories or discover missing transit visas at check-in.

**What it costs them:**  
- Thousands of dollars in emergency ticket repurchases when denied boarding due to missing transit visas.
- Exorbitant airport excess baggage fees ($150–$300+ per suitcase).
- Wasted days stranded in foreign transit airports during airline schedule disruptions without advocate representation.
- Severe anxiety and stress during what should be joyful family reunions or relaxing holidays.

**Emotional tension:**  
Travellers feel intense dread about making a costly paperwork mistake, fear being separated from family members in unfamiliar foreign airports, and feel helpless when corporate call centres refuse to take accountability for flight changes.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:**  
- **Flight Centre NZ & House of Travel:** Major nationwide retail travel agencies. *Fall short because:* Higher retail fee markups, frequent staff turnover, and less focused expertise on specialized diaspora routes and transit visa intricacies.
- **Ethnic & Subcontinental Travel Agencies in Auckland (e.g., Travelbiz, Travel Smart):** Local agencies serving similar diaspora corridors. *Fall short because:* Some lack modern digital quotation tools, maintain inconsistent customer service responsiveness, or lack transparent pricing policies.

**Secondary:**  
- **Online Booking Giants (Skyscanner, Webjet, Expedia, Trip.com):** Fast digital comparison. *Fall short because:* Zero personalized advice, hidden add-on costs, no proactive transit visa checks, and notoriously frustrating customer support during schedule changes.

**Indirect:**  
- **Direct Airline Websites (Air New Zealand, Singapore Airlines, Emirates, Qantas, Qatar Airways, Air India):** Direct booking with the carrier. *Fall short because:* Inability to create cross-carrier multi-airline itineraries, inability to book consolidated net fares, and rigid customer service.
- **Travellers attempting full DIY planning:** Relying on travel forums and blogs. *Falls short because:* High risk of missing regulatory updates, changing baggage allowances, and transit visa policies.

**Glossary:**

| **IATA Accredited** | Certified by the International Air Transport Association, permitting direct airline ticketing and verifying professional financial and operational compliance. |
| **Transit Visa** | An official entry permit required by certain countries (e.g. Australia, UAE, USA) even if the traveller is only passing through the airport between connecting flights. |

| **VFR Travel** | "Visiting Friends and Relatives"—the primary travel motivator for diaspora communities flying long-haul back to their countries of origin. |
| **Interline Agreement** | A commercial agreement between different airlines allowing passengers to book a unified ticket with baggage checked across multiple carriers. |
| **Baggage Allowance** | The specific weight (e.g. 2x 23kg or 30kg) and piece restrictions applied to checked luggage by international airlines. |
